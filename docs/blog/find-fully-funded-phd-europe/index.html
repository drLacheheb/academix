How to Find Fully Funded PhD & Postdoc Positions in Europe (2026 Guide)

Published by drLacheheb • 8 min read

One of the biggest advantages of pursuing an academic research career in Europe is the employment model: in many European nations, doctoral candidates and postdoctoral fellows are salaried employees rather than tuition-paying students.

This guide breaks down the primary European funding systems, public salary scales, and actionable steps to discover fully funded vacancies before application deadlines close.

1. The 4 Main European PhD & Postdoc Funding Models

A. Netherlands: The CAO-NU Employee Model

In the Netherlands, all university staff are covered by the Collective Labour Agreement for Dutch Universities (CAO-NU):

  • Salary Scale: Starts at €2,770 gross per month in year 1, automatically increasing annually to €3,539 gross per month in year 4.
  • Additional Allowances: 8% annual holiday pay (paid in May) and an 8.3% end-of-year bonus (paid in December), effectively providing ~14 months of salary per year.
  • Employee Status: Full pension contributions (ABP), paid parental leave, and 232+ annual vacation hours.

B. Germany: TV-L E13 Public Tariff Contracts

German universities and Max Planck / Helmholtz / Fraunhofer institutes hire doctoral and postdoc researchers under the TV-L (Tarifvertrag der Länder) pay scale:

  • PhD Level (TV-L E13): Usually advertised as 50% to 100% contracts depending on the field. STEM and engineering roles frequently receive 100% contracts (starting at >€4,300 gross per month), while social sciences or biology often range from 65% to 75% (~€2,800 gross per month).
  • Postdoc Level (TV-L E13/E14): Full 100% contracts with progressive experience tiers (Stufen) starting around €4,500–€5,400 gross per month.

C. France: CIFRE Industrial Doctorates & Doctoral Contracts

France offers two major fully funded routes:

  • Contrat Doctoral: Public university contracts with a minimum guaranteed gross salary of €2,300 per month (approx. €27,600/year), with optional supplements for teaching missions.
  • CIFRE (Conventions Industrielles de Formation par la Recherche): Co-funded by the French Ministry of Higher Education and an industrial partner. Candidates conduct PhD research embedded in a company R&D division, earning typical gross salaries of €2,400 to €3,200+ per month.

D. European Commission: MSCA (Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions)

MSCA Doctoral Networks (DN) and Postdoctoral Fellowships (PF) provide some of the most prestigious and generous funding in global academia:

  • Living Allowance: Set at a base rate of approx. €4,010 per month, multiplied by a country correction coefficient to reflect local cost of living.
  • Mobility Allowance: An additional €710 per month tax-free allowance for international relocation.
  • Family Allowance: Additional monthly allowance (€660/mo) for researchers with family obligations.

2. Why Most Candidates Miss Out on Top Positions

Unlike US programs that admit cohorts once per year in September, European universities recruit year-round as grants are awarded. A lab receiving an ERC grant in March will publish a 3-week vacancy call on EURAXESS or AcademicTransfer immediately.

If you only check job boards once a month, by the time you discover the listing, the application deadline has often already passed.
